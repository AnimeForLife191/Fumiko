# Account Credential Setup Guide

Fumiko connects directly to your email providers using two methods:
1. **Modern OAuth 2.0 with PKCE** *(Preferred for personal Microsoft accounts, or optional Google Cloud developer projects)*.
2. **Universal IMAP with App Passwords** *(Preferred for Gmail, iCloud, Fastmail, Yahoo, and custom servers)*.

Because Fumiko is local-first without backend servers, relays, or telemetry:
* **Zero Middlemen**: You communicate directly with provider servers over encrypted connections.
* **No Subscriptions or Fees**: Can't get much better when it's free.
* **100% Data Control**: Passwords and refresh tokens live exclusively in your operating system's native credential vault (`keyring`).

Setup takes **1 to 2 minutes**. Follow the step-by-step guides below.

---

## 1. Microsoft (Outlook / Hotmail via OAuth 2.0)

OAuth 2.0 with PKCE is the **preferred and standard method** for connecting Microsoft mailboxes. Because Microsoft deprecated Basic Authentication, personal Microsoft accounts cannot connect via IMAP App Passwords and use browser OAuth instead.

### Supported: Personal Microsoft Accounts (1-Click Setup)
* **Supported Domains**: `@outlook.com`, `@hotmail.com`, `@live.com`, `@msn.com`.
* **Setup**: Zero setup required. In Fumiko, simply go to **Add Account → Microsoft**, approve the prompt in your default browser, and confirm MFA on your phone if prompted.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Work and School Accounts Are Not Supported At This Time**  
> While Fumiko's architecture is built to support both personal and organizational accounts, enterprise and educational Microsoft 365 accounts cannot be connected at this time. Microsoft requires third-party applications to have official **Publisher Verification** (requiring a registered legal entity, D-U-N-S verification, and an authorized business domain) before granting access to organizational tenants. As an independent solo developer, commercial verification is simply not possible right now, and I apologize for the inconvenience! That said, if the project gains enough traction and support, I'll gladly jump through those legal hoops down the road!

## 2. Universal IMAP with App Passwords

IMAP with **App Passwords** is the fastest way to connect Gmail, iCloud, Yahoo, and Fastmail. It requires zero cloud console configuration, avoids 7-day token expirations, and keeps your primary account password completely safe.

> [!NOTE]
> **What is an App Password?**  
> An App Password is a 16-character passcode generated by your provider specifically for Fumiko. It allows Fumiko to read your mail without you ever sharing or exposing your real account password.
>
> *Fumiko automatically strips space groupings (like `abcd efgh ijkl mnop`), so you can paste codes directly into the app.*

---

### A. Google (Gmail) - Preferred Method
*Connecting Gmail via IMAP is strongly preferred over Google Cloud OAuth. It takes 30 seconds and eliminates Google's 7-day token expiration and unverified app warning screens.*

1. Ensure **2-Step Verification** is turned on in your **[Google Account Security](https://myaccount.google.com/security)**.
2. Open **[Google App Passwords](https://myaccount.google.com/apppasswords)**.
3. Name your app (e.g., `Fumiko`) and click **Create**.
4. Copy the generated 16-character code.
5. In Fumiko:
   * Select **Add Account → IMAP**.
   * Click the **Gmail** preset button (auto-fills `imap.gmail.com:993`).
   * Enter your Gmail address and paste the 16-character App Password.
   * Click **Connect**.

---

### B. Apple iCloud Mail
*Apple strictly requires App-Specific Passwords for third-party IMAP access.*

1. Go to **[account.apple.com](https://account.apple.com)** and sign in.
2. In the **Sign-In and Security** section, select **App-Specific Passwords**.
3. Click **Generate an app-specific password** (label it `Fumiko`).
4. Copy the generated 16-character password.
5. In Fumiko:
   * Select **Add Account → IMAP**.
   * Click the **iCloud** preset button (auto-fills `imap.mail.me.com:993`).
   * Enter your iCloud email address and paste the App-Specific Password.
   * Click **Connect**.

---

### C. Yahoo / AOL Mail
1. Sign in to your **[Yahoo Account Security](https://login.yahoo.com/account/security)** page.
2. Scroll to the bottom and click **Generate app password**.
3. Enter `Fumiko` as the app name and click **Generate password**.
4. In Fumiko:
   * Select **Add Account → IMAP**.
   * Click the **Yahoo** preset button (auto-fills `imap.mail.yahoo.com:993`).
   * Enter your Yahoo email address and paste the generated App Password.
   * Click **Connect**.

---

### D. Fastmail
*Fastmail provides native, read-only third-party app tokens out of the box.*

1. In Fastmail, go to **Settings → Password & Security**.
2. Under **Connected apps & API tokens** (or Integrations), click **Manage app passwords → New App Password**.
3. Name it `Fumiko`, grant it **Mail (Read-only)** access, and generate the key.
4. In Fumiko:
   * Select **Add Account → IMAP**.
   * Click the **Fastmail** preset button (auto-fills `imap.fastmail.com:993`).
   * Enter your Fastmail address and paste the generated App Password.
   * Click **Connect**.

---

### E. Custom / Self-Hosted IMAP
1. In Fumiko, select **Add Account → IMAP**.
2. Click **Custom Server**.
3. Enter your mail server's hostname (e.g., `mail.example.com`).
4. Enter your secure TLS port (standard is `993`).
5. Enter your full email address and mailbox password.
6. Click **Connect** (Fumiko runs a live TLS connection test before saving).

## 3. Google OAuth 2.0 (Advanced / Optional)

*If you prefer using Google's REST API over IMAP, you can register your own personal Google Cloud project.*

1. Open the **[Google Cloud Console](https://console.cloud.google.com)** and create a new project (e.g., `Fumiko Email`).
2. Go to the **[API Library](https://console.cloud.google.com/apis/library)**, search for **Gmail API**, and click **Enable**.
3. Under **Google Auth Platform**:
   * **[Audience](https://console.cloud.google.com/auth/audience)**: Set User Type to **External**, and add your email under **Test users**.
   * **[Scopes](https://console.cloud.google.com/auth/scopes)**: Click **Add or Remove Scopes**, filter for Gmail API, and select `.../auth/gmail.readonly`.
4. Go to **[Credentials](https://console.cloud.google.com/apis/credentials)**:
   * Click **Create Credentials → OAuth client ID**.
   * Select Application type: **Desktop App**.
   * Name it `Fumiko Desktop` and click **Create**.
5. Click **Download JSON** (or copy the Client ID and Secret).
6. In Fumiko:
   * Navigate to **Settings → OAuth Credentials**.
   * Under Google, click **Import credentials.json** (or paste your keys) and click **Save Credentials**.
   * Navigate to **Add Account → Google** to connect.

> [!NOTE]
> **The 7-Day Expiration in Google Cloud (Testing Mode)**  
> Google automatically expires refresh tokens every 7 days for projects in **Testing** status. 
> 
> If you want permanent tokens, there are two options:
> 
> 1. **Use IMAP (Method 2.A):** It's way easier and avoids this completely.
> 2. **Publish the app yourself:** You can go through the process of publishing the app, though it's a bit of a hassle since Fumiko uses a restricted scope (`gmail.readonly`), which triggers Google's full verification requirements:
>    * **App Name & User Support Email**
>    * **An Authorized Domain**
>    * **An Application Homepage & Privacy Policy**
>    * **Developer Contact Email**
>    * **Written justification** explaining why the app needs to read emails
>    * **Screencast video (YouTube link)** showing the app's login flow, making sure the browser's address bar clearly displays your Client ID, followed by how the app uses the emails it reads
>    * **Possible Security Assessment (CASA Tier 2):** This is the most expensive part at roughly $540 to $1,800 USD per year (renewed annually). I think Fumiko might be excluded from this since CASA is only required for apps that store or transmit Gmail bodies, metadata, or tokens to an external server (and she keeps everything strictly on your local device), though I'm still not completely sure.
> 
> ...And that's why Google's OAuth requires your own credentials. If Fumiko picks up enough traction and support though, I will definitely look into getting this officially done, as it could knock out Microsoft verification at the same time!

## 4. Microsoft Custom Azure Registration (Advanced / Optional)

*Personal Microsoft accounts connect 1-click without this. If you prefer to register your own Azure developer credentials, follow these steps.*

### Step-by-Step Setup

1. Open the **[Azure Portal](https://portal.azure.com)** and navigate to **[App registrations](https://portal.azure.com/#view/Microsoft_AAD_RegisteredApps/ApplicationsListBlade)**.
2. Click **New registration**:
   * **Name**: Enter `Fumiko` (or any preferred name).
   * **Supported account types**: Select **Accounts in any organizational directory (Any Microsoft Entra ID tenant - Multitenant) and personal Microsoft accounts**.
3. Under **Redirect URI (optional)** on the same page:
   * Select platform: **Public client/native (mobile & desktop)**.
   * Enter URI: `http://127.0.0.1/`
4. Click **Register**.
5. Navigate to **API permissions**:
   * Click **Add a permission → Microsoft Graph → Delegated permissions**.
   * Verify or add the following three permissions:
     * `Mail.Read`
     * `User.Read` *(mandatory for personal accounts to retrieve your profile/address)*
     * `offline_access` *(mandatory to receive refresh tokens)*
6. Return to the **Overview** blade, copy your **Application (client) ID**, and paste it into Fumiko under **Settings → OAuth Credentials**.

> [!TIP]
> **Do NOT enable "Allow public client flows"**  
> In Azure under Authentication, keep *"Allow public client flows"* set to **No**. Fumiko uses standard loopback authorization code with PKCE, which does not require legacy non-interactive flows.

## 5. How Fumiko Protects Your Credentials

Fumiko maintains physical isolation between non-sensitive metadata and sensitive secrets:

* **Non-Sensitive Data (SQLite)**: Email addresses, display names, sync cursors, IMAP host/port settings, and custom Client IDs are stored locally in an indexed SQLite database.
* **Authentication Secrets (OS Keyring)**: Raw IMAP App Passwords, long-lived OAuth refresh tokens, and Google Client Secrets are stored exclusively in your operating system's native credential store:
  * **Windows**: Windows Credential Manager
  * **macOS**: Apple Keychain
  * **Linux**: FreeDesktop Secret Service API / Keyutils
* **Ephemeral Memory Tokens**: Short-lived OAuth access tokens remain strictly in RAM with a 50-minute TTL and are never written to disk or diagnostic logs.

## Troubleshooting

### Microsoft: "Need admin approval" or sign-in blocked
You are attempting to sign in with an organizational, university, or corporate Microsoft 365 account. Fumiko only supports **personal** accounts (`@outlook.com`, `@hotmail.com`, `@live.com`). Work and school accounts cannot be connected due to institutional enterprise restrictions.

### IMAP: "Authentication failed / Invalid credentials"
* **Check for App Password usage**: Regular account passwords will fail on Gmail, iCloud, and Yahoo. You must use a dedicated App Password.
* **Ensure 2FA is active**: Providers block App Password generation unless Two-Factor Authentication is enabled on the account.

### Google: "This app isn't verified"
This only appears when using custom Google Cloud OAuth. Click **Advanced → Go to Fumiko (unsafe)**. Because you created the developer project, you are simply authorizing your own private client to read your own mailbox.
